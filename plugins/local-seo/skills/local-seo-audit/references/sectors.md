# Sectors and audiences

Apply the section for the brief's sector, and the section for its audience. These add checks; they don't replace the six areas.

## Sectors

### Clinics and healthcare
- Scan treatments, not the clinic name. Phrase them the way patients search.
- Check every clinic and practitioner profile at the address. Practitioner profiles only for public-facing practitioners with their own patients; a solo practitioner behind a branded clinic shares one profile. Extra profiles split reviews and compete with the clinic.
- One page per treatment: what it is, who does it, how long it takes, price guidance, where.
- Review replies must never confirm someone was a patient or discuss their care. Provide reply templates that are warm and say nothing about the person.
- Treatment naming: in the UK, prescription-only medicines (for example botulinum toxin) can't be advertised to the public. Name the concern or the consultation instead, and flag wording for the clinic's compliance lead.
- Review requests: SMS with a one-tap link as the patient leaves, one email follow-up.

### Trades and home services
- Grids sized to how far they travel; check where the jobs actually come from.
- Service-area businesses: the verified address anchors rankings even when hidden. Ranking far beyond it comes from relevance and reviews, not service area settings.
- Pages per service, and a small number of area pages with real jobs, photos and reviews from that area. No town-swap pages.
- Lead quality: are calls for the jobs they want? Split calls and forms by service where possible.
- Keywords in the business name are a common reason trade profiles get suspended.

### Hospitality and leisure
- Searches guests make before they arrive: "<type> near <landmark>", "<type> with <feature>".
- Photos, menus, prices and the booking link on the profile.
- Track direct bookings against platform bookings, compared with the same season last year.
- Reviews: ask at checkout, then one email the day after with a direct link.

### Professional services
- Firm and partner profiles mapped so they don't compete. One profile per office.
- Practice-area pages that match how clients search ("divorce solicitor", not "family law services").
- No keywords added to the firm's name.

### Retail and automotive
- Department and product searches scanned separately.
- Departments (for example sales and service at a dealership) can have their own profiles where they genuinely run separately: own entrance, hours or phone line.
- Product and inventory data where it feeds Google.

### Anything else
Use the six areas as written. Ask one extra question if the sector has rules that affect what it can say (finance, legal, health).

## Audiences

### Owner (one location)
Plain English, written for the owner and their staff. Owners named as "you", "your web person", "your agency". Keep the first-three list to things they can start this week.

### Multi-location brand
- Audit the sample in depth, then look for the same fault across the estate.
- Group findings by cause, not by branch: "14 locations link to the brand homepage" is one fix.
- Check location pages exist for every branch, with unique local detail.
- Note which locations are strong and which weak, and what the strong ones have in common.
- For 21 or more locations, say plainly that a full estate view needs a scanning tool, and what to scan.

### Agency client
- White-label: no mention of Mani or this skill in the report body.
- Owners named as "agency team" and "client".
- Add a short "For the account team" section at the end: what to present first, what the client must approve, what the agency can do without the client.
- The next-steps block is for the agency, not the client.
