# Orient: what the audit needs, and where to find it

Find each piece yourself first. Ask the user only for what you can't find, in one message, and say why you need it.

| The audit needs | Look here first | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| What the business sells, and to whom | The website, the repo, the brief | Decides the money searches and which competitors count. |
| The searches that bring revenue (up to 5) | Site navigation and service pages, Search Console queries, the Business Profile's search terms, the brief | The audit measures these, not the business name. A search people type to find any provider beats one only existing customers use. |
| Where customers come from | Site copy, the profile's service area, location pages, the user | Sets the area to measure. Ask if unclear: it decides what a "good" result looks like. |
| Customers come to them, or they go to customers | Address shown or hidden on the profile, site copy | Changes how rankings are measured and what reach is realistic. |
| How a customer becomes revenue | Calls, booking widgets, forms, checkout on the site | Decides what to track and what "working" means. |
| Rules on what it can say | The sector, any regulator named on the site | Shapes copy and review replies. Find the rule before writing. |
| One location or many | Location pages, store finder, the profile | Many: audit a sample (strongest, weakest, typical) in depth, then group faults by cause across the rest. |
| Who the audit is for | The conversation, the brief | Owner, in-house team or agency client changes the wording and the owners named, not the method. |
| The profile | The Maps listing, a connected tool, or the user | Name, categories, services, link, hours, photos, reviews. |
| Who is winning | Measured in area 1 | The 1 to 3 businesses that take over where this one drops out. Every comparison uses them. |
| What's already been tried | `local-seo-log.md`, the user | So you don't recommend what failed, or undo what worked, without knowing why. |
| Anything that changes the order | The user | Suspension, a recent move or rebrand, a website change, a deadline. A suspended profile is fixed before anything else. |

## Writing the brief

Write what you learned to `local-seo-brief.md` in plain lines. Keep it under 40 lines and factual: the business, the shape from the lens, money searches, area, competitors, audience, data available, constraints. Other skills read it, so use plain labels rather than a fixed form. If a brief exists, update it rather than replacing it.

## Very large estates

For 20 or more locations, say plainly that a full estate view needs a scanning tool, audit a sample of three in depth, and look for the same fault repeating: the same category, the same landing-page pattern, the same review gap. One fix to a repeated cause beats twenty local fixes.
