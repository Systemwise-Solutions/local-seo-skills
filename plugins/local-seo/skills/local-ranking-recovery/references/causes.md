# Causes and their signatures

Each cause leaves a different pattern: which searches drop, on which surface, in what shape, and what stays put. Match the pattern, then run the confirming check. The confirming check is what moves confidence from medium to high.

## 1. Read the pattern first

| Pattern | Points to |
|---|---|
| Gone for the business name too | Suspension, a duplicate taking its place, a merge, or a name change Google hasn't accepted. Eligibility first. |
| Name still fine, services gone | Category change, filtering, a competitor, or the website link now pointing at a weaker or dead page. |
| Maps and local pack down, organic steady | A profile cause, filtering or competition. Not the website. |
| Organic down, local steady | The website or a Google update. Hand to technical SEO. |
| Everything down on one date | The website (launch, noindex, changed NAP), or a confirmed update. Check the site first. |
| One search down, another up | A category or relevance shift: the profile now looks more like something else. |
| All branches at once | Something shared: the website, a brand-wide edit by head office or an agency, an update. |
| One branch | That branch's profile, its location page, or a competitor in its patch. |
| Cliff | An edit, a suspension, a launch, a filter. |
| Slope | A competitor building up, reviews going stale, an update rolling out, seasonality. |
| Shrinking radius: still top 3 at the door, gone further out | Prominence or proof lost against competitors (reviews, a category, a page), or a new competitor between the business and the edge of its area. |

## 2. Business Profile causes

| Cause | Signature | Confirming check | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary category changed | Cliff within days of the edit. The old category's searches fall; searches matching the new category may rise. Name search unaffected. | The competitors now holding the lost searches use the old category; searches for the new category rose. | Put the primary category back to the one that matches the money searches. Keep the other as a secondary only if the business really offers it. Expect Google to re-check the profile; have verification evidence ready. |
| Name changed | Name searches can wobble; a keyword-stuffed name may have given a short lift and then a suspension. | Edit history; signage photos. | Name exactly as on the signage and registration. |
| Address or pin moved | The strong radius moves or shrinks around the new point. | Compare old and new grids: the centre of strength moved. | Correct the address and pin to where the business is. If it really moved, rebuild there and update listings. |
| Service area or hidden address change | Usually little ranking effect; can prompt re-verification or suspension if a storefront hides an address customers visit, or a service-area business shows an address they can't. | The notice or verification request date. | Match the profile type to reality: storefront if customers visit, service-area if not. |
| Hours changed | Fewer results at times the business now shows as closed. Calls drop at those times only. | Search at those hours from the business. | Correct hours. Special hours for holidays. |
| Website link changed or broken | Clicks drop, rankings may drift down over weeks. | Click the link on the profile: does it land on a live page that matches the money searches? | Link to the right live page (the location or main service page). |
| Suspension, hard or soft | Hard: gone from Maps and name searches, often overnight. Soft: still visible, can't edit or edits don't go live. | The notice from Google. | See suspension.md. |
| Verification request | The profile stops showing or editing pauses until verified. | Email or profile notice. | Complete it, with the evidence in suspension.md ready. |
| Duplicate merged or removed | Review count jumps or falls on one date; name or category may have changed to the other listing's. | Review counts over time; what the surviving listing now shows. | Ask Google support to correct the surviving listing; keep evidence of the right details. |
| New person with access | Edits nobody remembers making. | Who is on the profile's user list; remove anyone who shouldn't be there. | One owner, a change log, and no edits to name, address or category without a reason. |

## 3. Website causes

Local results lean on the page the profile links to, and on the site describing the same business. Common causes after a website change:

- **Location or service pages removed or merged.** Redesigns often fold every location into one page and lose every local ranking at once.
- **Redirects missing or all pointing to the homepage.** Most migration losses come from a handful of URLs that mattered and weren't redirected one to one.
- **The profile's website link now redirects or 404s.**
- **noindex or robots blocks** left on from staging.
- **Name, address or phone changed on the site**, so it no longer matches the profile.
- **LocalBusiness structured data dropped** in the rebuild.

Confirming check: Search Console by page and query, before and after the launch date. The pages that lost clicks are the list to fix. Fetch the old URLs and see where they land.

Fix order: the profile's website link and the page it points to first, then redirects for every URL that earned traffic, then the location pages, then NAP on the site.

## 4. Google updates

Only name an update as a cause when its dates are confirmed from Google's own Search Status Dashboard or announcement, and the drop's shape fits (usually a slope over the rollout, often on organic as well as local). Many local ranking shifts are never announced. If the only evidence is "someone said there was an update", record it unconfirmed and keep looking for a cause the business controls. Recovery from an update is slower and not guaranteed; the useful response is the same proof and prominence work as an audit, not undoing recent edits.

## 5. Reviews

| Cause | Signature | Check | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reviews removed | Total count falls on a date; can come with a slope in rankings. | Count history; ask whether the removed reviews came from a burst, staff, family or an incentive. | Don't try to replace them in a burst. Start a steady request routine (SMS with a one-tap link within the hour the job ends, one email follow-up). |
| Negative run | Rating falls over days; calls fall more than rankings (people still see you, but choose others). | Review dates and text. | Reply to each, calmly and specifically. Report fake, spam or competitor reviews through the profile, with evidence. |
| Stale reviews | Slope over months; competitors' reviews are newer. | Newest review date against competitors'. | A routine, not a campaign. |

## 6. Competitors

| Cause | Signature | Check | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| New competitor nearby | Lost points cluster on the side where it opened; often a slope. | Grid results over time: who took over at the lost points. | Close the proof gap with them: category, services, page, reviews. |
| Competitor moved closer | Same as above, from a known date. | Their address history. | Same. Proximity can't be undone; win where you're close enough. |
| Competitor keyword-stuffed its name | They jump above you for the searches in their name. | Their Maps name against their signage, website and Companies House or equivalent record. | Google's redressal process for reporting listings that break the guidelines, with the evidence. Don't copy it: it gets profiles suspended. Strengthen your own proof meanwhile. |

## 7. Filtering

Google tends to show one of several similar businesses that sit at the same address or very close together in the same category, and hide the rest for that search. A filtered business often still shows for its name and reappears when the map is zoomed right in.

Typical cases: two practices in the same building, a franchisee and head office, a practitioner listing and the clinic, an old listing for a previous business at the address, a duplicate.

Check: search the money search from the business's own location; zoom in and out; see who shows instead. Look up every listing at the same address.

Fix: remove or merge genuine duplicates and old listings. For two real businesses side by side, make each distinct: its own primary category where that's true, its own entrance, phone, website page and reviews. There is no setting that turns the filter off.

---

## 8. Measurement

Before any ranking diagnosis, check the drop is real: a changed or failed call tracking number, a profile disconnected from the reporting tool, a site launched without Analytics, Google not yet reporting the latest days (profile data lags by several days). If rankings held and only one metric fell, suspect measurement first.

## 9. Site-wide drops: the short version

When organic dropped too, or every location dropped at once, it is usually the website. If SEO for AI Agents is installed, hand over to its `rescue` job; it uses `seo-access-migrations` for redirects and URL changes and `seo-access-reach` for crawling and indexing. If not:

1. Pin the date and the pages: Search Console clicks by page, before and after.
2. Fetch the top lost URLs: do they redirect one to one to the right new page, or 404, or go to the homepage?
3. Check for noindex, robots blocks and wrong canonicals on the key pages.
4. Check the pages each profile links to still exist and still say what and where.
5. Fix redirects and lost location pages first: they hold most of the value.

Point the user to github.com/manibharij/seo-for-ai-agents for the full method.
