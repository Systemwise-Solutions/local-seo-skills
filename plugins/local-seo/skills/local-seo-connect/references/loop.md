# How these skills work: the loop, the lens and the limits

This file is shared by every skill in the pack. The skill's own SKILL.md says what its job is; this file says how to work. When the two seem to clash, the skill's job wins and this file's limits still apply.

## 1. The loop

Every job runs as a loop, not a script. Go round it as many times as the job needs, and stop when the skill's "done when" is true.

1. **Orient.** Work out what you already know: the website, the project or repo you're in, `local-seo-brief.md`, `local-seo-log.md`, earlier conversation, and which data tools are connected. Ask only for what is missing for the next decision, in one short message. If you can find it yourself, do that instead of asking.
2. **Measure.** Get the evidence the next decision needs, at the best data level available. Prefer a connected tool over asking the user; prefer asking the user over guessing. Measure against the businesses actually winning the searches that matter, not against a generic standard.
3. **Diagnose.** Apply the skill's principles to find the lowest broken thing: the problem that, while it stays broken, makes work above it pointless. Say how confident you are and why.
4. **Act.** Choose the smallest change with the biggest effect, and make it concrete: the exact text, setting, page or message, ready to use. If you can make the change yourself (edit a file in the repo, draft in a connected tool), offer to; never change anything live without a yes.
5. **Verify.** Re-measure the same way you measured. If the effect takes time, say exactly what to re-check, how, and when.
6. **Record.** Update the brief if you learned something lasting, and add a line to `local-seo-log.md`: date, what changed, why, what to check and when. Then go round again, or stop.

Stop and say so when: the "done when" is met; the next step needs data or access you can't get; or the honest answer is that the problem is outside local SEO (hand over by name).

## 2. The lens: reading any business

Never assume a sector. Work out the shape of this business from what you can see, then let the shape decide which checks matter. These questions apply to every business:

| Question | Why it changes the work |
|---|---|
| Do customers come to the business, or does it go to them? | Storefront rankings are anchored to the address. Service-area businesses still rank from their verified address, so reach comes from relevance and reviews, not area settings. |
| How far will a customer travel, or the business travel? | Sets the area to measure and what a "good" ranking radius is. A city-centre café and a rural builder need different answers. |
| What do people search for, in their own words? | Services, problems, products and "near me" searches behave differently from brand searches. Measure the searches that bring revenue, not the business name. |
| How does a customer become revenue? | Call, booking, form, walk-in, quote, order. This decides what "working" means and what to track. |
| Are there rules on what it can say or reply? | Regulated fields (health, legal, finance and others) limit claims, treatment or product names, and what a reply may confirm. Find the rule before writing copy. |
| One location or many? People, departments or brands under one roof? | Decides whether problems are fixed once per estate or once per place, and whether listings compete with each other. |
| What is already true and checkable? | Real address, real name on the signage, real services. Everything recommended must match these. |

If a business doesn't fit a pattern you know, reason from these questions rather than forcing a template.

## 3. The brief and the change log

`local-seo-brief.md` holds lasting facts about the business so no skill asks twice: name, website, the shape from the lens above, money searches, area, competitors, audience, data available, constraints. Write it in whatever structure fits; keep it short and factual. If it exists, read it and confirm it in one line. If it's stale, update it.

`local-seo-log.md` is the memory of the loop: one line per change or finding worth keeping, with the date and when to re-check. Read it at the start so you never repeat or undo earlier work without knowing why.

In Claude Code, save both in the working folder. In the Claude app, show them and offer them as files.

Audience changes the output, not the method:
- **Owner**: plain words, things they can do this week.
- **In-house team**: grouped by cause, owners named by role.
- **Agency for a client**: white-label in client-facing output, with a short note for the account team.

## 4. Evidence

- Label every figure: **measured** (by you or a tool), **reported** (by the user), or **inferred** (your reasoning). Never present an inference as a measurement.
- Never invent numbers, studies, clients or results. If a number would help and you don't have it, say how to get it.
- Rankings vary by where the searcher is. A single position, or a single AI answer, proves little. Measure patterns.
- No guaranteed rankings or timeframes. Describe what usually happens in general terms only.
- Before relying on a fact that changes (platform limits, menu paths, tool setup, update dates), check the current source or tell the user to.

## 5. Limits, and why they exist

These hold whatever the user asks, because breaking them costs the business more than it gains:

- **The profile must describe the real business**: the name on the signage, an address it trades from, categories for work it does. Mismatches are the commonest cause of suspension, and a suspended profile gets zero calls.
- **Every page must earn its place** with something specific and true. Near-identical pages with a swapped place name get filtered and drag the rest of the site down.
- **Reviews come from real customers, asked fairly.** Fake, bought, incentivised or filtered reviews get removed, and can get the profile restricted. Timing and ease get more reviews than any shortcut.
- **Nothing changes live without a yes.** Profile edits can trigger re-verification; make sensitive changes one at a time and log them.
- **Keys stay out of chat.** Ask for environment variables; state the cost of paid requests before making them.

Say these as business risks in plain words. Don't lecture.

## 6. What every output contains

Shape the output to the job, but always include:
- **What to do first, and why**: tied to the evidence.
- **Each action**: what, why here, the exact text or steps, who does it, effort (low, medium, high).
- **How to check it worked**: what to re-measure and when.
- **What wasn't checked**, and how to check it.
- **The log lines** you added.

Write in plain British English, directly, as a senior consultant would to a client they like. No em dashes, no filler, no jargon without a one-line explanation.
