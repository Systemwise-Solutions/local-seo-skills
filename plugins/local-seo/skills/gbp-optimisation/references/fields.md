# Every field

Each field: the principle and its reason, what makes it a change, and the risk where there is one. This is the order of thinking; the order of editing is in change-order.md. Field names and options change, so check what the profile offers now.

## 1. Name

**Principle:** exactly the name on the signage, the website and the registration, nothing added, because Google checks the profile against what it can see in the real world.

**Change when:** the profile name carries services, towns, "24/7", "near me", taglines or separators that aren't part of the real name.

**Risk:** keywords in the name are one of the commonest reasons profiles get suspended, often added by a past agency years ago. Removing them is the first change in the log, made on its own. Categories, services and the landing page carry the same relevance without the risk.

**Competitors doing it:** don't copy. If a stuffed name is beating the business, report it through Google's Business Redressal Complaint Form (find it in Business Profile Help) with evidence: a photo or Street View of the real signage, the registered company name, the website's own name.

## 2. Address and service area

**Principle:** an address the business operates from and staffs, because that is what Google verifies and what customers rely on. Customers visit: show it. They don't: hide it and set a service area. Both: show it and set a service area.

**Change when:** the address is a virtual office, an unstaffed co-working desk, a PO box or mailbox service, or an old address after a move; or the address shows when customers can't visit.

**Risk:** an address the business doesn't operate from gets profiles suspended, and they can't be reinstated because the business genuinely isn't there. If that's the only address, say plainly the profile isn't safe as it stands; the realistic options are real staffed premises or a service-area profile verified where they really operate.

**Service area:** describes where they work. It doesn't widen where they rank, because the verified address anchors proximity even when hidden. Set it to the real area, not every town in the region.

**Moved:** edit the address on the existing profile. A second profile splits reviews and can affect both.

## 3. Primary category

**Principle:** the category for the work that earns most, checked against the businesses beating them for that search, because it is the strongest relevance signal the owner controls.

**Change when:** category_gap.py shows most winners use a different primary for the money search, or the current one is broader than the work. For example, a venue whose bookings are mostly weddings listed under a general "Event venue" while the winners use "Wedding venue"; or a repair shop listed as a retailer when repairs are the revenue.

**Don't follow blindly:** if the winners' primary matches work the business doesn't want, keep the one that matches the money. Ranking for the wrong job brings calls nobody wants.

**Risk:** changing it can cause movement for a few days while Google re-evaluates, and can prompt a review. Change it only on evidence, on its own, and log the date.

## 4. Secondary categories

**Principle:** only work they genuinely do and want more of, because each extra category dilutes the primary a little. Check the current limit in the picker; few businesses need the maximum.

**Change when:** a category most winners use matches real, wanted work and is missing; or the profile has categories for work they've stopped. Remove those.

If the primary changes, the old primary often becomes a secondary, if they still do that work.

## 5. Services

**Principle:** one service per money search, named the way customers search, each with a short description, because services are how the profile matches searches the categories don't cover. Use Google's predefined services where they fit and custom ones for the rest.

**Change when:** a money search has no service (category_gap.py lists these); services use internal or vague names; or towns are listed as services.

Source the wording from the profile's search terms, autocomplete and the winners' services, and pick the phrasing the search terms show, because that is what real customers typed.

## 6. Products

**Principle:** products show packaged offers with a real price or "from" price and a photo, because price is what people look for before they get in touch.

**Change when:** winners show products and the business shows none, or prices appear nowhere on the profile. Use real prices only, never invented ones. If the business won't publish prices, use a product for the free consultation, quote or trial instead.

## 7. Description

**Principle:** first sentence says what the business does and where, for the person choosing; then what it's known for, who does the work, the area, and proof that can't be faked. Check the current character limit. No links, offers, prices, keyword lists or phone numbers, because Google rejects or ignores them.

**Change when:** missing, generic, out of date (old services, old area), stuffed with towns, or containing links or promotions.

It doesn't drive rankings much. It helps people choose, and AI assistants read it. Write it in copy.md's shape.

## 8. Hours

**Principle:** the hours the phone is answered or the door is open, because wrong hours lose customers, produce angry reviews, and "open now" searches filter out businesses shown as closed. Set holiday hours ahead of every public holiday, and "more hours" (for example appointment-only, delivery or call-out hours) where the category offers them.

**Change when:** "Open 24 hours" without someone answering; hours differ from the website; holiday hours weren't set last season.

## 9. Attributes

**Principle:** every attribute that's true, because customers filter on them: accessibility, payments, online booking, onsite services, languages, and identity attributes only if the owner wants them shown. They vary by category.

**Change when:** true attributes are missing, or an untrue one was added by Google or the public.

## 10. Phone

**Principle:** a local number that's answered, the same as the website. With call tracking: the tracking number as primary and the main number as additional, so the known number still matches across the web.

**Change when:** the number differs from the website or main directories, or goes to voicemail in opening hours.

## 11. Website link

**Principle (shared with the audit):** point to the page that matches the money searches, because the landing page is part of what the profile ranks on.
- One location, one dominant offer: link to that page.
- Several money searches: the homepage only if it clearly leads with them and links to each; otherwise the top revenue page.
- Multi-location: each profile to its own location page, never the brand homepage.

Add the same tracking parameters on every profile (for example `?utm_source=google&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=gbp`) so analytics can separate profile visits.

**Change when:** it points to a homepage listing everything, a dead page, or the brand homepage from a branch.

**If the right page doesn't exist:** the change waits. Building it is `local-pages` work; give the short version if that skill isn't installed.

## 12. Booking, ordering or quote link

**Principle:** whatever turns a visitor into revenue (book, order, request a quote, reserve) should be one tap from the profile and work on a phone, because that is where most profile visitors are. If the business uses a booking or ordering system Google integrates with, connect it through that provider's own instructions.

**Change when:** empty, broken, or pointing at the homepage.

## 13. Photos

**Principle:** real, recent and recognisable, because photos help people choose and recognise the place. Show what a customer would see: the entrance and signage if they visit, the work or the product, the people, the vehicles if they travel. Set the logo and cover on purpose.

**Change when:** few photos next to the winners, stock images, nothing recent, or customer photos showing the wrong business.

They aren't a ranking lever. Add a few each month rather than a hundred at once. Don't spend time adding GPS data to photos. If there are rules on what can be shown (people's faces, results, regulated products), follow them. The shot list is in copy.md.

## 14. Posts

**Principle:** post when it helps a customer: a seasonal reminder, something new, real work done, an event, an offer with a date. Weekly is plenty if posts are useful.

**Not a ranking lever.** Never put posts first. No phone numbers, emails or links in the text, because Google rejects them; use the button.

## 15. Q&A

Google has been changing and reducing this feature, so check what the profile shows now. Where it exists: answer open questions from the business account, correct wrong public answers, report spam. Where the questions customers ask on the phone aren't answered anywhere, answer them in the description, services and the website FAQ, which assistants also read.

## 16. Duplicates and individual listings

**Find them:** search Maps for the business name, the phone number, old names, old addresses, and the names of individual people who work there.

- **Duplicate at the same place:** ask Google support to remove it, or merge it if it has reviews worth keeping. Don't delete the verified profile to start fresh: it loses the reviews.
- **Old address after a move:** mark it as moved through support rather than leaving it live.
- **People under one roof:** a separate profile is legitimate only for a public-facing professional with their own clients, because otherwise listings split reviews and compete with the main profile. A lone professional working under a business brand shares one profile, in the form "Business name: Person's name". Ask support to mark listings for people who've left as moved or closed.
- **Departments or brands under one roof:** separate profiles only where they are genuinely separate to a customer (own entrance, hours or phone line). Brand or franchise rules may govern the name; check before any change.

These go to support in parallel; they don't change the main profile.

## 17. Multi-location consistency

- One profile per staffed location, never for areas without premises, because those get suspended.
- The same naming pattern on every profile; a location descriptor only if it's on that branch's signage.
- The same category set for the same type of branch, unless a branch genuinely does different work.
- Each profile links to its own location page, with the same tracking parameters.
- Hours, holiday hours and attributes per branch, set centrally ahead of holidays.
- Store codes and labels so the estate can be managed and reported by branch.
- Pilot a category or naming change on one or two branches, let it settle, then roll out, so a bad change hits two profiles rather than all of them.

## 18. Edits from Google and the public

Google and the public can suggest edits, and Google sometimes applies them. During the settle period, check weekly for "Google updated" notices and reject untrue ones. Afterwards, monthly.
