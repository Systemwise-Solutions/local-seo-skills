## 1. Name

**Rule:** exactly the name on the signage, the van, the website and the company registration. Nothing added.

**Change when:** the profile name has services, towns, "24/7", "emergency", "near me", taglines or separators that aren't part of the real name.

**Risk:** keywords in the name are one of the most common reasons profiles get suspended, often added by a past agency years ago. A suspended profile drops out of Maps entirely until reinstated. Removing them is the first change in the log, made on its own. Categories, services and the landing page carry the same relevance without the risk.

**Competitors doing it:** don't copy. If a stuffed name is beating the business, report it through Google's Business Redressal Complaint Form (find it in Business Profile Help) with evidence: a photo or Street View of the real signage, the Companies House name, the website's own name.

## 2. Address and service area

**Rule:** an address the business trades from and staffs. Customers visit: show it. Customers don't visit: hide it and set a service area. Both: show it and set a service area.

**Change when:** the address is a virtual office, a co-working desk nobody staffs, a PO box or mailbox service, or an old address after a move. Or a service-area business shows an address customers can't visit.

**Risk:** a virtual office or an address they don't trade from gets profiles suspended and can't be reinstated, because the business genuinely isn't there. If the only address they have is a virtual office, say plainly that the profile isn't safe as it stands, and that the realistic options are a real staffed premises or a service-area profile verified at an address they really operate from.

**Service area:** describes where they work. It doesn't widen where they rank: the verified address anchors proximity even when hidden. Set it to the real area (towns or postcodes they cover), not every town in the county.

**Moved:** edit the address on the existing profile. Never create a second profile.

## 3. Primary category

**Rule:** the category that matches the job that pays most, checked against the three businesses beating them for that search.

**Change when:** category_gap.py shows most competitors use a different primary for the money search, or the current one is broader than the work ("Medical clinic" when competitors are "Dermatologist"; "Plumber" when the money is in boiler installs and the winners are "Heating contractor").

**Risk:** changing it can cause movement for a few days while Google re-evaluates, and it can prompt a review. Change it only on competitor evidence, on its own, and log the date.

## 4. Secondary categories

**Rule:** only work they genuinely do and want more of. Google allows up to 9 alongside the primary; few businesses need that many.

**Change when:** a category most competitors use matches real work the business does and is missing; or the profile has categories for work they've stopped (bathrooms they no longer fit, a treatment no longer offered). Remove those.

If the primary changes, the old primary often becomes a secondary, if they still do that work.

## 5. Services

**Rule:** one service per money search, named the way customers search, each with a short description. Add the predefined services Google offers for the category where they fit, and custom services for the rest.

**Change when:** a money search has no service named for it (category_gap.py lists these); services use internal or vague names ("Heating", "Laser"); or towns are listed as services.

Source the wording from the profile's search terms (SearchOps or the performance tab), autocomplete and the competitors' services. "Boiler service" and "boiler servicing" are both fine; pick the one the search terms show.

## 6. Products

**Rule:** use products to show priced, packaged offers with a photo: "Combi boiler installation from £X", "Initial physio assessment, 45 minutes, £Y". Each links to its service page.

**Change when:** competitors show priced products and the business shows none, or prices are listed nowhere on the profile. Prices are what people look for before they call. Use the business's real prices or "from" prices only; never invent them.

## 7. Description

**Rule:** up to 750 characters. First sentence says what the business does and where, for the person choosing. Then what it's known for, who does the work, the area covered, and proof that can't be faked (years trading, registrations, accreditations). No links, no offers or prices, no keyword lists, no phone numbers.

**Change when:** it's missing, generic, out of date (old services, old area), stuffed with towns, or contains links or promotions.

Description doesn't drive rankings much. It helps people choose, and assistants read it. Write it in copy.md's shape.

## 8. Hours

**Rule:** the hours the phone is answered or the door is open. Holiday hours set ahead of every bank holiday. "More hours" (for example appointment-only, or emergency call-out hours) where the category offers them.

**Change when:** "Open 24 hours" without a person answering 24 hours; hours differ from the website; no holiday hours were set last season.

**Why:** wrong hours lose calls, produce angry reviews ("said open, no answer"), and searches with "open now" filter out businesses shown as closed.

## 9. Attributes

**Rule:** every attribute that's true: accessibility, payments, online appointments, onsite services, languages, and identity attributes only if the owner wants them shown. They vary by category.

**Change when:** true attributes are missing, or Google or the public added one that isn't true (check for "Google updated this" notices).

## 10. Phone

**Rule:** a local number that's answered, the same as on the website. With call tracking: the tracking number as primary, the main number as additional, so the business's known number still matches across the web.

**Change when:** the number differs from the website or the main directories, or goes to voicemail in opening hours.

## 11. Website link

**Rule (shared with the audit skill):** it should point to the page that matches the money searches.
- One location with one dominant service: link to that service's page.
- Several money searches: link to the homepage only if it clearly leads with them and links to each service page; otherwise link to the top revenue service.
- Multi-location: each profile links to its own location page, never the brand homepage.

Add tracking parameters so Analytics can separate profile visits, for example `?utm_source=google&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=gbp`. Keep the same parameters on every profile.

**Change when:** it points to a homepage listing everything, a dead page, or the brand homepage from a branch.

**If the right page doesn't exist:** the change waits. Build the page first (what it is, who does it, how long, price guidance, where, a call or book button at the top). Page building is a separate job; point to the pack's page skill if installed, or give the short version.

## 12. Booking link

**Rule:** a link to the page where a customer can book or request a quote, that works on a phone in a few taps. If they use a booking system Google integrates with, connect it through that provider's own instructions.

**Change when:** empty, broken, or pointing at the homepage.

## 13. Photos

**Rule:** real, recent and recognisable. Signage and entrance (helps people find it and supports verification), inside, the team, the work, vans with livery. A logo and a cover photo set on purpose.

**Change when:** few photos next to competitors, stock images, nothing from the last year, or customer photos that show the wrong business.

Photos help people choose; they aren't a ranking lever. Add a few each month rather than a hundred at once. Don't spend time on adding GPS data to photos. The photo plan is in copy.md.

## 14. Posts

**Rule:** post when it helps a customer: a seasonal reminder, a new service, real work done, an event, an offer with a date. Weekly is plenty if posts are useful.

**Not a ranking lever.** Posts help people looking at the profile. Never put them first. No phone numbers, emails or links in the post text (Google rejects them); use the button instead.

## 15. Q&A

Where the profile still shows Questions and answers (Google has been changing and reducing the feature, so check what's on the profile now): answer any open questions from the business account, correct wrong answers from the public, and report spam. Where the questions customers ask on the phone aren't answered anywhere, answer them in the description, services and the website's FAQ instead, which assistants also read.

## 16. Duplicates and practitioner listings

**Find them:** search Maps for the business name, the phone number, old names and old addresses, and each practitioner's name.

- **Duplicate of the same business at the same place:** ask Google support to remove it, or to merge it if it has reviews worth keeping. Don't delete the verified profile to "start fresh": it loses the reviews, and a second profile for the same business can affect both.
- **Old address after a move:** mark the old listing as moved through support, rather than leaving it live.
- **Practitioners (clinics, law firms, accountants):** separate profiles only for public-facing practitioners who see their own patients or clients. A solo practitioner behind a branded clinic shares one profile, named in the form "Clinic name: Practitioner name". Practitioners who've left: ask support to mark the listing as moved or closed. Extra listings split reviews and compete with the main profile.

These go to support in parallel with the profile edits; they don't change the main profile.

## 17. Multi-location consistency

- One profile per staffed location, never for areas without premises.
- The same naming pattern on every profile: the brand as on the signage. Add a location descriptor only if it's on that branch's signage.
- The same category set for the same type of branch, unless a branch genuinely does different work.
- Each profile links to its own location page, with the same tracking parameters.
- Hours, holiday hours and attributes per branch, set centrally ahead of every bank holiday.
- Store codes and labels so the estate can be managed and reported by branch.
- Roll a category or naming change out on one or two pilot branches first, wait for them to settle, then the rest. One cause, one fix across the estate: "14 locations link to the brand homepage" is one change.

## 18. Edits from Google and the public

Google and members of the public can suggest edits, and Google sometimes applies them. During the settle period, check the profile weekly for "Google updated" notices and reject any that aren't true. Afterwards, monthly.
