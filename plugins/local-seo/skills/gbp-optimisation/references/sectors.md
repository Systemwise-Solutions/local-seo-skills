# Sectors and audiences

Apply the section for the brief's sector and the one for its audience.

## Sectors

### Clinics and healthcare
- Services named as patients search for the treatment or the concern ("mole removal", "knee pain physiotherapy"), not clinical terms.
- In the UK, prescription-only medicines (for example botulinum toxin) can't be advertised to the public. Name the concern or the consultation ("Anti-wrinkle consultation"), and flag every instance of wording for the clinic's compliance lead. You're not giving legal advice.
- Practitioner listings: only public-facing practitioners with their own patients. A solo practitioner behind a branded clinic shares one profile in the form "Clinic name: Practitioner name". Check every listing at the address.
- Description and posts: no claims about results. Registrations (GMC, HCPC, GDC, NMC, CQC) are proof worth stating.
- Photos: no patient faces or results without written consent, and none for prescription-only treatments.
- Primary category tends to be the specialty ("Physiotherapist", "Dermatologist", "Skin care clinic", "Cosmetic dentist"), not "Medical clinic".

### Trades and home services
- Primary category for the job that pays: "Plumber" and "Heating contractor" compete for different searches.
- Service-area businesses: hide the address if customers don't visit. The verified address still anchors rankings. Service area settings don't widen them.
- "24/7", "emergency" or town names in the business name are a common reason trade profiles get suspended. Use hours, "more hours" and services instead.
- Products with "from" prices for packaged jobs (boiler installs, rewires, EICRs). Registrations (Gas Safe, NICEIC, NAPIT, TrustMark) in the description.
- Photos of finished jobs, vans with livery, the team. Monthly.
- Profiles only for real, staffed premises. Profiles in towns they cover without premises get suspended.

### Hospitality and leisure
- Categories and attributes guests filter on (outdoor seating, dog friendly, step-free access).
- Menu, prices and booking link on the profile. Hotel profiles show amenities and prices: keep them accurate.
- Photos change with the season.

### Professional services
- One profile per staffed office. Partner profiles only for public-facing practitioners, set so they don't compete with the office.
- Services that match how clients search ("divorce solicitor", "self assessment tax return").
- No keywords in the firm's name. Regulated wording (SRA, ICAEW) flagged for the compliance lead.

### Retail and automotive
- Departments (sales and service at a dealership) can have their own profiles only where they're genuinely separate: own entrance, hours or phone line.
- Products for best sellers. Manufacturer brand rules may govern the name: check before any name change.

## Audiences

### Owner
Plain English. The change log is for them to work through themselves with manager or owner access. Keep the first three to things they can do this week.

### Multi-location brand
Work on a sample, then group changes by cause across the estate. Name owners by role (marketing manager, branch manager). Pilot sensitive changes on one or two branches first. Say plainly that checking every branch's competitors needs a scanning tool.

### Agency client
White-label: no mention of Mani or this skill in the client-facing output. Owners named as "agency team" and "client". Add "For the account team": which changes need the client's approval (name, category, anything about prices or regulated wording), and which the agency can make with manager access. The closing line goes to the agency only.
