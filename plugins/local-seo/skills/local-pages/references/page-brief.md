## 2. Page brief template

```markdown
### <Page name>

- URL: <slug>
- Target search: <main search>, also <2 to 4 close variants from search terms>
- Searcher: <who they are and what they want, one line>
- Title (under 60 characters): <Service in Place | Business name>
- Meta description (under 155 characters): <what, where, one proof point, the action>
- H1: <service and place, plainly>
- Opening lines (2 or 3 sentences): <what you do, where, the one thing that makes you the safe choice>
- Above the fold on mobile: call button (tel: link), book or quote button, price guidance or "from" price, star rating with review count
- Sections:
  1. <What the service involves, how long it takes>
  2. <Prices or price guidance>
  3. <Proof: recent jobs with photos, where>
  4. <Who does it: team, credentials>
  5. <Areas covered, linking to location pages that exist>
  6. <Questions customers ask, 3 to 5, answered briefly>
- Proof to gather: <photos, reviews to quote with permission, prices, credentials: each marked supplied or needed>
- Internal links out: <service pages, location pages>
- Internal links in: <which pages should link here, with suggested anchor text>
- Schema: <LocalBusiness subtype + Service, see schema.md>
- Facts needed from owner: <list>
```

Location page briefs add: the area's specifics (housing stock, access, local rules), jobs done there with dates and photos, reviews naming the place, travel time or response time to it, and links to each service done there. They never carry an address unless there is a staffed premises in that town.

## 3. What goes on a page that ranks locally

- **What and where, plainly**, in the title, H1 and the first two sentences.
- **The service itself**: what it involves, who does it, how long it takes, price guidance where the owner will give it.
- **Proof from that place**: projects, team, photos and reviews that mention the area.
- **Practical details**: parking, transport, hours, coverage, response times.
- **Questions customers really ask**, answered in two or three sentences each.
- **Structured data that matches the profile**, and links to and from related pages.
- **Quotable statements** for AI assistants: plain sentences stating what the business does, where, for whom, at what price, with which credentials. Assistants quote facts, not slogans.

## 4. Writing rules

- Write for the person searching on a phone, who wants to know if you do it, where, how much, and whether you're any good.
- Short paragraphs, two or three sentences. Headings that are questions or plain labels.
- Specific beats general: "We fitted 14 combi boilers in Headingley this year, mostly replacing back boilers in terraces" beats "We serve Headingley with quality boiler services".
- Prices: a real "from" price or range, with what changes it. If the owner won't publish prices, say what the quote depends on and how fast it comes.
- Reviews: quote real reviews only, with the customer's first name and area, with permission. Never write or paraphrase a review.
- No keyword stuffing. Use the town name where a person would: in the H1, the opening, the proof and the directions. Not in every sentence.
- No em dashes, no filler, no "nestled in the heart of".
- Placeholders in square brackets for anything not supplied, listed at the end.

## 5. Mobile: call and book above the fold

Check on a phone-width screen (about 390px wide). Before any scrolling, the visitor should see:
- What and where (the H1).
- A call button that is a real `tel:` link, using the same number as the profile (or the website tracking number if call tracking is set up).
- A book, quote or enquiry button that works.
- One proof point: star rating and count, a "from" price, or a credential.

Keep heavy hero images and sliders out of the way. A fast page with the phone number, prices and the booking button up front does more than a redesign.
