# Sectors and audiences

## Sectors

### Clinics and healthcare
- Ask as the patient leaves: SMS with a one-tap link, then one email follow-up. Reception mentions it at payment.
- Requests never mention the treatment or condition. The patient decides what to say.
- Replies follow the safe templates in replies.md, always. Never confirm someone was a patient, name the practitioner who saw them, or refer to their care, even when they did.
- Never repeat the name of a prescription-only medicine (for example botulinum toxin) in a reply or request. In the UK those can't be advertised to the public, and a reply is public.
- Practitioner profiles split reviews. If patients review individual practitioners' profiles instead of the clinic's, point the link at the clinic's profile (the gbp-optimisation skill covers which listings should exist).
- Reviews on Doctify, iWantGreatCare or similar help trust and are among the sources AI assistants read. For the map pack, Google reviews matter most in Mani's experience. Don't split the request: one link per message.
- Flag wording for the clinic's compliance lead. This isn't legal advice.

### Trades and home services
- The engineer or fitter sends the SMS before leaving the drive, or the job app sends it when the job is closed.
- Ask them to mention the job and the area: it builds relevance for the places they want to rank in, beyond the base.
- Checkatrade, TrustATrader or Trustpilot reviews help trust. For the map pack, Google reviews matter most. Pick one link per message; Google by default.
- Leave a card with the QR code on site, and print it on the invoice.

### Hospitality and leisure
- Ask at checkout or as the bill is paid; one email the day after the stay or visit, with the direct link.
- Reviews that mention the area, landmarks and features help with the searches guests make before they arrive.
- Tripadvisor and booking platforms have their own review flows. Keep the Google request separate.

### Professional services
- Ask when the matter is complete and the client is happy with the outcome, not mid-matter. Everyone at that point, not only the happy ones.
- Confidentiality applies to replies, as for clinics.
- Partners' individual profiles: point the link to the office profile unless the practitioner profile is the one meant to rank.

### Retail and automotive
- QR at the till and on the receipt. For dealerships, separate links for sales and service if they have separate profiles.

## Audiences

### Owner
Plain English, set up this week, with the messages saved where they'll actually be sent from.

### Multi-location brand
- One link and QR code per location. A central automation, branch-specific wording.
- Report by branch, never as an average: which branches have the routine running and which don't.
- Name owners by role: branch manager owns asking, a central person owns replies or sets the rota.
- Regulated groups: one set of approved reply templates, signed off by compliance, used everywhere.

### Agency client
- White-label: no mention of Mani or this skill in client-facing output. The client's name and voice in every message.
- Owners named as "agency team" and "client". The client must approve the messages and the reply templates; the agency can set up the links, QR codes and tracking.
- Add "For the account team": what the client must approve, who replies (agency or client), and how removals are logged.
